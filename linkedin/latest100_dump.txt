The Prior Authorization API under CMS-0057-F takes effect January 1, 2027. A little over three months.

Almost everything written about it is about speed — requests moving through an API instead of a fax queue. From the review chair, that's not the part that matters.

The load-bearing line is in CMS's own fact sheet. The API has to be populated with the payer's list of covered items and services, and it has to identify documentation requirements for prior authorization approval.

That's the criteria checklist. Published, and queryable before the note is written.

Most denials I see aren't clinical disagreements. The care was right, and the note answered a different question than the criteria asked. That checklist has always existed. It just lived on the payer's side of the wall.

Two things to stay clear-eyed about.

The API carries the request. It does not make the decision. A submission that still doesn't establish what the criteria ask gets denied faster, not less.

And the scope is narrower than the coverage suggests. Impacted payers are Medicare Advantage organizations, Medicaid and CHIP fee-for-service and managed care, and QHP issuers on the federally facilitated exchanges. Medicare fee-for-service isn't on that list. Neither are commercial group plans.

One thing already in effect that fewer offices use than should: since 2026, impacted payers have had to give a specific reason for a denied prior authorization — by portal, fax, email, mail, or phone. If your denials still say "not medically necessary" and stop there, that's a question you can ask today, not in January.
